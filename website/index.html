Dubplate is a free, open-source two-deck alternative to rekordbox and Traktor. Cue the next track in your headphones, line up the beats and bring it into the mix when the phrase turns.

Get Dubplate ↓

Cue it before the room hears it.

Keep deck A playing to the room. Cue deck B in your headphones, then nudge it into time by ear or use beat sync. When the phrases line up, bring in the new track and swap the bass with the LOW EQ knobs. Or turn on Smart Fader and move the crossfader to glide between tempos and swap the bass as you blend.

Still digging for a track? Press P to preview the selected track in your headphones before loading it onto a deck.

Give the transition another bar.

A four-beat loop buys you another bar for the handoff. Beat Jump moves the playhead by a set number of beats, and Hot Cue takes you straight to a marked point in the track.

Catch the last hit.
Tap the echo pad on a snare and release it. The repeats fade over the track as it keeps playing.
Make space for the next track.
Select the Sweep preset in Smart CFX and turn the CFX knob to the right to narrow the outgoing track to a band-pass. With Echo out on the faders enabled, close the channel fader quickly to leave a half-beat echo behind.
Build your own FX bank.
Start with Classic, Afro house or Amapiano. Edit any of the 32 Pad FX assignments, from the length of a roll to how an echo rings out when you release the pad.

Leave the drums in. Take the vocal out.

With stems available for a track, adjust its drums, bass, melody and vocals separately. Mute the outgoing vocal before the next one comes in, or put an acapella over the other deck’s instrumental.

Use the sampler for sounds from outside the two tracks. Drop a clap on the break, or bring in the one-bar Shaker groove and let it follow the master tempo. The sampler has four banks of 16 slots for your own sounds and built-in percussion, chords and DJ effects.

Press REC or Command + Shift + R to capture the master output after the limiter. The recording includes your blends, samples and echo tails.

Get Dubplate.

For Macs with Apple silicon. Download the disk image, open it, then drag Dubplate into Applications.

Download for Mac ↓ Release notes ↗

Version 0.1.0 is signed with Apple Developer ID and has not been notarized by Apple. Gatekeeper may block this version from opening.

Desktop app via Homebrew

brew tap lvndry/dubplate
brew trust lvndry/dubplate
brew install --cask dubplate

If your Homebrew version lacks brew trust, skip that line. For the CLI, use brew install --formula dubplate after adding the tap.

App and CLI via shell installer

curl -fsSL https://github.com/lvndry/dubplate/releases/latest/download/install.sh | sh

The installer verifies both downloads. Run it again to upgrade. Read the installer

Before the first track.

Can I start with a folder of music?

Yes. Choose Add music folders on first launch. Dubplate includes subfolders and analyses tracks in the background. You can load a track while the rest of the library is being analysed.

What happens to my rekordbox collection?

Import reads a copy of the collection and its analysis files. Your playlists, cues and beat grids come across. Dubplate saves subsequent edits separately, so you can keep using rekordbox. The import requires your rekordbox database key. Library setup guide

Which DJ controllers work with Dubplate today?

The Pioneer DDJ-FLX4 is the first controller with a built-in mapping. Broader controller support is a project goal. You can also mix with the on-screen decks and keyboard. See the current controller mapping

Can I mix with just a keyboard?

Yes. In Perform, use Space to play or pause the active deck, Shift+Space for deck B, and the left and right arrows for the crossfader when the track list has focus. Press ? to see the full shortcut list.
